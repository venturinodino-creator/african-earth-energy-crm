A perspective on our approach to sustainability

AECI's sustainability approach is centred on how the Group creates, preserves and protects value over the long term. In this context, sustainability
is not treated as a parallel agenda, but as a core business discipline that supports competitiveness, resilience and responsible growth. Our 2025
performance demonstrates continued progress in embedding environmental, social and governance priorities into decision making, operations
and oversight.

Sustainability governance

Governance of sustainability related risks and opportunities follow the IFRS S1 four pillar logic of governance, strategy, risk management and metrics
and targets. The Board retains ultimate accountability for strategy, risk and performance, supported by its committees.

Management accountability is structured through Group functions and business units. ESG topics are
incorporated into the combined assurance framework, ensuring that cross functional teams including
Group functions such as Internal Audit, Compliance, Sustainability, ESG, EHS, IT, Tax, Treasury and external
assurance providers, work together to keep key risks within Board approved appetite and to strengthen the
underlying control environment.

The combined assurance committee meets quarterly and reports into the relevant Board committee.

Our risk culture is shifting from a primarily compliance driven model to a more proactive, embedded way
of working. Recent culture surveys included questions on how frequently employees consider risk when
executing transactions and making operational decisions.

Early results are positive and are being used as evidence that risk awareness is improving across
the Group.

Overall, the period from 2024 to 2025 marks a visible transition.
The foundations of ERM have been put in place, risk appetite has been
defined, ESG indicators are built into dashboards and incentives and
the Group is beginning to see the benefits of a more mature, integrated
risk culture.

Case study

Plant Heath: Transforming agriculture with purpose

AECI Plant Health strengthened its position as a strategic growth pillar. The business sharpened its
focus on integrated crop solutions that combine biologicals, crop nutrition, soil health technologies
and digital agronomy tools such as Khula!. Strategic emphasis shifted from selling individual products
to partnering with farmers, contract farming partners, depots and crop advisors to deliver end-to-
end support that improves yields, soil condition and regulatory compliance. This approach reflects
our double materiality lens: safeguarding farmer livelihoods and food security while protecting
natural resources and long-term enterprise value.

Plant Health continued to build out its regional and export presence, leveraging research-led
formulations, local field trials and regulatory approvals to adapt offerings to specific soils, crops
and climate conditions. Technical teams worked alongside distributors and advisors to ensure that
product selection and application were optimised for local realities, positioning Plant Health as
a trusted agronomy partner rather than a commodity input supplier.

The operating pressures experienced in 2025 are reinforcing our focus on delivering                     Agriculture with purpose
resilient, cost efficient and sustainable solutions for customers operating in increasingly
complex and resource constrained environments. In 2026, the Group will continue to
prioritise circular economy initiatives, improved resource efficiency and
data enabled decision making to help customers manage cost, availability and
environmental risk across their operations. Evolving operating models and partnerships are
being shaped around explicit ESG outcomes, enhancing transparency, traceability and long-
term value creation across value chains. Continued innovation in products, services and
digital advisory capabilities, supported by responsible supply chain collaboration and
market led partnerships, positions the Group to support customer productivity, regulatory
compliance and climate resilience, while advancing sustainable growth aligned with
our Net Zero by 2050 commitment and shared value ambitions.

Strengthening resilience through decarbonisation

We are committed to developing a clear and credible decarbonisation pathway in support of our Net Zero by 2050 ambition. This pathway is underpinned by realistic short- and medium-term priorities, complemented
by longer-term, aspirational initiatives. It reflects our intent to systematically identify, quantify and address future potential emissions reduction gaps through a combination of operational improvements, technology
interventions, commercial and pricing mechanisms and, where appropriate, selective market based instruments. In doing so, we are strengthening the resilience of our business while positioning the Group for a
transition to a lower carbon future.

Our decarbonisation agenda is a strategic driver of value creation. It supports the sustainability of our operations, enables our customers' transition journeys and reinforces continued access to capital in an environment
of increasing climate regulation. Our primary focus remains on reducing Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions, while enhancing data quality and expanding our influence over the most material Scope 3 categories. We continue
to make measured progress against this pathway, advancing initiatives grounded in implemented projects and a robust pipeline of future opportunities, as we work towards neutralising our overall climate impact over
the long term.

Our Net Zero Pathway transition is how we move from commitment to action. It translates the 2050 ambition into a practical, phased roadmap that links site level initiatives
      to Group-wide emission reduction outcomes. The pathway maps opportunities across our global operations, classifying them into Priority 1, 2 and 3 initiatives and
                                                                    sequencing them over short-, medium- and long-term horizons.
